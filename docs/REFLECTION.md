# Individual Reflection: decID

*Draft: edit it so it's in your own voice and reflects your experience. Keep it to one page.*

**What went right.** I started from a broad idea in Assignment 1 (decentralized identity) and narrowed it into a product with a clear customer problem: businesses must check age, and people don't want to hand over their whole ID. Using an AI development tool (Claude Code), I got a working prototype quickly. I was then able to iterate like a real startup:
- every time I pushed back ("the UI looks AI-generated", "the test wallet doesn't make sense"), the next version got better;
- the tool's automated tests and security tests gave me confidence the product actually works, not just that it looks right;
- researching competitors (Yoti, TruAge, Apple Wallet ID) sharpened the positioning: decID builds on what people already carry and adds the proof businesses need.

**What went wrong.**
- **Too wide.** I followed too many interesting directions (blockchain anchoring, government IDs, fraud detection, biometrics, health records). The product got deep but also wide, which made it harder to explain simply.
- **Late user research.** Some features were built before I had customer data, so my research came after much of the building instead of before it.
- **Confusing UI.** A few features confused me as a user at first (the demo wallet, the multiple demo people). If I was confused, a real customer would be too.
- **Budget.** I ran low on AI credits and had to prioritise late in the project.

**What I'd do better next time.**
- Talk to 5–10 bartenders, store owners and students in the first week, and let their answers decide the first features.
- Set a scope and budget for each sprint in Trello, and say no to features outside the beachhead (alcohol delivery and venues).
- Write the pitch story early, so every feature has to earn a place in it.
- Test the demo on someone who has never seen it before presenting.

**Other.** The biggest lesson: AI makes building fast, so the scarce resource becomes judgment. That means choosing the right problem, the right customer and the smallest version that proves the idea. I'd also want legal input early (trademark conflict with "decide", privacy law, and health-data rules) before going further.
